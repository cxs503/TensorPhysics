## 1. Introduction — what is mapped, and why it is hard

Coupling a finite-element heat-conduction model (unstructured P1 triangles) to a
lattice-Boltzmann natural-convection solver (uniform Cartesian lattice) requires a
*scalar* exchange operator `FEM → LBM`. Two properties are non-negotiable:

1. **global conservation** — `∫_Ω T dA` on the source mesh equals `Σ_j |C_j| T_j` on
   the target lattice (the error budget of this node is 1 %);
2. **no new extrema** — `min(T_target) ≥ min(T_source)` and `max(T_target) ≤ max(T_source)`.
   A remap that manufactures a 1.2 K hot spot inside a field whose maximum is 1.0 feeds
   the LBM solver with non-physical buoyancy.

A third, practical, requirement is locality: the overlap table is built once from
geometry, after which every exchange is a sparse matrix–vector product, i.e. the cost
is proportional to the number of overlapping element/cell pairs and independent of the
number of fields exchanged.

`tensorphysics.field_exchange` already owns the opposite direction (LBM/DEM tractions →
FEM nodal forces, `docs/EXCHANGE_CONSERVATIVE_MAPPING.md`). This node adds the scalar
FEM → LBM direction plus the conservative-remap machinery:
**source cell integrals → overlap-volume weighting → limited reconstruction → bounded
conservative redistribution**.

## 2. Method

### 2.1 Source: P1 heat conduction on an unstructured mesh

`mesh.py` builds a jittered triangulation of the rectangle: interior nodes are
displaced by up to `0.25 h`, boundary nodes slide *along* their edge (so the boundary
stays exactly the rectangle), and every quad is split along a diagonal chosen such that
the two triangles tile the quad exactly. The last point is the *geometry gate*: area
closure to `1e-12`, no inverted element, min/max element-area ratio recorded. Jittering
is what makes the mesh non-conforming to the Cartesian target — without it the test
case would be trivial.

The temperature solves

    -∇·(k ∇T) = f   in Ω,    T = g on Γ_D,    k ∂T/∂n = 0 on Γ_N

with exact constant element gradients and a degree-2 source quadrature. Two source
problems are used:

* manufactured field `T = sin(πx) sin(πy)` with `f = 2π²k T` (analytic
  `∫ T dA = 4/π² = 0.405285`), Dirichlet 0 on the whole boundary;
* Rayleigh–Bénard conduction state `T = 1 - y` with adiabatic side walls, which a P1
  method reproduces *exactly* (a discretisation check as well as the physical
  initial condition of the coupled run).

### 2.2 Overlap table (geometry only, built once)

Every element is clipped against every lattice cell it touches (Sutherland–Hodgman,
reusing `field_exchange.mapping.clip_rect`). Because the P1 field is linear inside an
element,

    ∫_{C_j∩T} T dA = |C_j∩T| · T(centroid of C_j∩T),

so the source integrals assemble into a sparse matrix `M` with
`(M · T_nodes)_j = ∫_{C_j} T dA`. The mapped areas sum to the source area (minimum
coverage fraction `1.000000000000`), hence

    Σ_j |C_j| T_j = ∫_Ω T dA   — an algebraic identity of the operator, not a tolerance.

### 2.3 The three operators

| operator | reconstruction | conservation | monotonicity |
|---|---|---|---|
| `average` | cell average of the P1 field | exact (identity) | exact (convex combination) |
| `linear` | overlap-weighted element-wise linear extrapolation to the cell centroid | **broken** by the reconstruction | **broken** (new extrema) |
| `bounded` | `average + ψ (linear − average)` with a Barth–Jespersen factor `ψ ∈ [0,1]`, then bounded conservative redistribution | restored to round-off | exact (bounds = nodal range over the overlapping elements) |

The redistribution (`remap.conservative_repair`) moves the limiter's integral defect only
into cells that still have slack towards their local bound: the increment is
`β·slack` with `β = min(1, |need| / Σ(slack·area))`. This is exact in one sweep when the
capacity suffices, never leaves the bounds, and reports `feasible=False` instead of
silently violating a bound when it does not. Clipping alone can never achieve both
properties — the clip *is* what creates the defect.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Smooth field (FEM mesh 32×32 → lattice 64×64)

Source solver: linear-system residual `1.338e-14`, integral `0.404186` vs analytic
`0.405285` (`0.2711 %`), nodal L2 error `0.1431 %`.

| operator | conservation `|defect|/∫` | cells above / below source range | L2 error vs analytic cell average | limiter defect before repair |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| average | 0.000e+00 | 0 / 0 | 0.305 % | — |
| linear | 3.899e-04 | 0 / 6 | 0.2916 % | — |
| bounded | 2.747e-16 | 0 / 0 | 0.3124 % | 3.910e-04 |

Note on accuracy: at the level of *cell averages* both `average` and `bounded` are
second order, because the overlap integral is the exact integral of the P1 interpolant
and the reconstruction error is `O(h²)` as well; the reconstruction buys pointwise
second order at the cell centroid and, more importantly, the two operators differ in
the other two columns — `average` cannot represent a peak that lives between two
elements, while `bounded` resolves it without producing new extrema. For the smooth
field the limiter clips six cells and costs `0.007` percentage points of L2 — the price
of the guarantee, not a defect.

### 3.2 Patch test (consistency)

A linear field `T = 0.7 − 1.3x + 2.1y` is reproduced by every operator stage with
maximum deviation `7.438e-15` — all three operators reproduce the exact cell average of
a linear field, i.e. the exchange does not bias a smooth solution.

### 3.5 Coupled demonstration: FEM conduction → LBM natural convection

Source: FEM conduction state on the LBM box `[0,2] × [0,1]` (64×32 jittered triangles,
`∫T dA = 1.000000` vs analytic `1.000000`, `2.22e-14 %`), remapped onto the
65×33 thermal-LBM lattice (node spacing `dy = 1/32`, wall-to-wall height
`32` lattice units).

* exchange: `|∫_FEM − ∫_LBM| / ∫` = `0`, mapped field vs the analytic
  conduction profile L2 = `2.327e-13 %`, out-of-range cells `0`;
* transport in an insulated box (400 steps): relative drift of `Σ T` =
  `3.138e-14`, wall budget `0`, balance mismatch `3.365e-11`;
* with the isothermal plates driven by the *mapped* wall temperatures
  (1200 steps): the mapped field is the exact discrete steady state to
  `7.550e-15` and the Nusselt number is `1.000000000000` (analytic value 1);
* natural convection at Ra = 2e+04, Pr = 0.71 (8000 steps,
  τ_f = `0.650`, τ_g = `0.711`): Nu = `3.892`, max|u| = `0.080`
  (Mach `0.08`), `T ∈ [0.0152, 0.9848]`, energy-balance closure
  `6.860e-10`.

Energy bookkeeping in the LBM closes *by construction*: BGK collision conserves the
zeroth moment and streaming only permutes populations, so every change of `Σ T` is
attributable to the `(g_BC − g_outgoing)` budget of the wall nodes, which the solver
accumulates and compares against the measured history (`balance mismatch` above).

## 7. Caveats, limitations, and what was *not* done

* **Plate convention.** The plates are driven at the *mapped* wall-node temperatures.
  Driving them at the analytic 1/0 instead leaves the mapped field `O(dy)` away from the
  discrete steady state (`≈ 0.030` in T): a boundary-convention artefact that is
  reported rather than hidden. Which is correct depends on the coupling interface
  contract; both are supported (`hot_wall`/`cold_wall` accept scalars or per-node
  arrays).
* **Lateral boundaries are periodic** (textbook Rayleigh–Bénard setup). On-node side
  walls were tried during development and abandoned: the bounce-back pairing at a
  corner node has five outgoing but four incoming links, which leaks mass at
  round-off-plus level and destroys the exact energy audit. An interior-wall variant is
  future work.
* **Scalar fields only, 2-D only, static meshes only.** Momentum/traction exchange stays
  in `field_exchange`; 3-D and moving meshes are not implemented.
* **The LBM is a demonstration consumer, not a validated solver** (no turbulence model,
  no immersed boundaries, first-order wall treatment). Its purpose is to prove that the
  exchanged field integrates into a solver whose energy budget is auditable.
* **Contact-area threshold.** Intersections smaller than `1e-14` of the mean element
  area are discarded; this is the origin of the round-off-level coverage deficit
  `1 − 1.000000000000`.
* **Mesh generator retries with reduced jitter** if the geometry gate fails (default
  `0.25 h`: jitter above ≈`0.25 h` can make neighbouring quads overlap).
* The reconstruction used for `bounded` extrapolates the *element-wise* linear field, so
  its accuracy is limited by the source discretisation; the refinement study confirms
  second order, but a genuinely higher-order source (P2) was not attempted.
