## 1. Case introduction

### 1.1 Physical background

A ship advancing through level ice crushes the ice sheet intermittently at the bow.
The sawtooth-shaped crushing force has a fundamental flaking frequency
`f_ice = U/s` (drift speed over flaking length) and a broadband tail; it drives the
hull-girder modes, and the vibrating shell radiates the low-frequency part of the
underwater noise field. The node implements this causal chain end to end

```
F_ice(t)  ->  modal superposition q_i(t)  ->  1/3-octave acceleration
                              \->  surface velocity -> Rayleigh far field
                                     -> 1/3-octave source level SL(f)
                                     -> propagation TL(f, r) -> RL(f, r)
```
with `E_crush = int F_ice U dt` as the mechanical energy input of the whole chain,
so that the acoustic conversion efficiency `E_rad / E_crush` is an output, not an
assumption. The applications served are the ISO 19906 ice-induced-vibration
assessment (habitability envelope) and marine-mammal noise exposure assessment
(range to threshold).

## 2. What was done (method)

1. **Modal basis** (`ice_vib_noise/modal.py`). Uniform free-free Euler-Bernoulli
   hull girder with analytic eigenfunctions reused from
   `tensorphysics.ice_vibration.modal`. The modal mass matrix and the generalised
   stiffness are integrated on a 4001-point grid and audited: the largest
   off-diagonal mass ratio is 7.24e-08 (criterion 1e-6), the largest
   off-diagonal stiffness ratio 2.26e-13, and the eigenvalue recovered from
   `K_ii/M_ii` reproduces `(2 pi f_i)^2` to 5.09e-08
   relative (a check that shapes and the frequency formula belong to the same
   boundary-value problem).
2. **Ice load** (`source.py`). Intermittent crushing: a renewal pulse train at
   `f_ice = U/s` with 8 % arrival jitter and 12 % log-normal amplitude scatter on top
   of the sawtooth shape, amplitude `p_c h w_c`. The load is projected on the mode
   shapes through a normalised half-sine bow contact distribution, giving the
   generalised forces `Q_i = g_i F_ice(t)` with g_i = 0.8928, -0.8187, 0.7466.
3. **Response** (`response.py`). Each modal equation is integrated with the exact
   closed-form transition for a piecewise-linear force (Nigam-Jennings), so the only
   error is the linear interpolation of the load between samples; a Newmark-beta
   integrator is kept as an independent cross-check in the tests.
4. **Radiation** (`radiator.py`). The hull bottom is a baffled planar patch grid with
   the normal-velocity distribution of the mode. The Rayleigh-Sommerfeld far field
   `p = i rho w/(2 pi r) exp(ikr) int v exp(-i k n.x) dS` gives, by angular integration,
   the radiation resistance `R_i(omega)` of every mode (used as radiation damping in
   the equation of motion and as the acoustic power weight) and the source spectrum in
   a fan of directions. No efficiency is fitted.
5. **Spectra** (`bands.py`). IEC 61260-1 exact base-10 1/3-octave bands
   (G = 10^0.1, edges = f_m G^(+-1/2)); band values are true integrals of the PSD, not
   single-bin samples.
6. **Propagation** (`acoustics.py`). Per spectral line 20 log10 r spherical spreading
   plus Thorp absorption; the omni-directional equivalent source level is the one
   propagated (a directional maximum with a spherical-spreading law would be
   inconsistent) and the band-centre approximation is reported separately.
7. **Criteria**. C1 zero-load negative control, C2 modal orthogonality, C3 energy
   closure + Parseval band closure, C4 linearity of the vibration->noise transfer
(which is what legitimises the envelope translation) and C5 idempotent digests.

**Toolchain.** pure Python/NumPy, deterministic (`seed=20261110`, no wall-clock in the physics), 26 unit/integration tests in
`tests/test_ice_vib_noise_chain.py`.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Modal and radiation properties

| mode | f_n [Hz] | m_n [t] | R_n(f_n) [N s/m] | zeta_struct | zeta_rad | DI at peak [dB] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.708 | 5750 | 26.46 | 1.50 % | 5.17e-07 | 11.80 |
| 2 | 1.953 | 5750 | 67.55 | 1.50 % | 4.79e-07 | 12.09 |
| 3 | 3.828 | 5750 | 2463 | 2.00 % | 8.9e-06 | 12.11 |

Radiation damping of the hull girder is 4-5 orders of magnitude below structural
damping — the physically expected result for a steel hull at `kL < 1` — while the
radiation *resistance* still controls the acoustic power. The 2-node mode radiates
like a dipole with a cancellation plane athwartships: the directivity index of the
fan maximum is 11.8 to 14.5 dB above the omni-directional equivalent.

### 3.2 Energy budget

| quantity | value [J] | share of E_crush |
|---|---|---|
| crushing energy E_crush | 7.95532e+07 | 1 |
| work on the hull structure W_in | 9.68033e+06 | 0.122 |
| structural dissipation | 9.15587e+06 | 0.115 |
| radiated acoustic energy (spectral) | 2200.24 | 2.77e-05 |
| radiated acoustic energy (ODE damping term) | 448.885 | 5.64e-06 |
| closure residual |W_in - sum| | 1760 | 0.000182 |

The crushing energy is the mechanical energy the ice cover absorbs (`int F U dt`);
only 12.17 % of it reaches the hull as structural work and only
0.0028 % leaves as underwater sound. The acoustic branch is therefore 2.27e-04 of the
input work, i.e. three orders of magnitude below the quadrature error of the
ledger, which is why the closure residual must be attributed carefully:

* `residual_with_ode_damping` = 3512 J (0.000363 of W_in) is the pure
  quadrature/integration error of the energy integrals;
* the reported `residual` = 1760 J (0.000182 of W_in) additionally contains the difference
  between the frequency-dependent radiation resistance used for the spectral
  acoustic power and the at-resonance resistance used in the equation of motion
  (E_rad,spectral - E_rad,ODE = 1751 J).

Both numbers are far inside the 1 % criterion and the attribution is explicit: the
residual is dominated by the radiation-model difference, not by lost energy.

The drift-speed sweep changes both the flaking frequency and the ice power input, so
`W_in` is not monotonic: work input is a phase-sensitive quantity that peaks when the
flaking frequency sits on a hull-girder natural frequency and collapses when the pulse
train is off-resonance. Doubling the structural damping from 1.5 % (design) to 3 %
reduces the worst acceleration ratio and lowers the source level — the expected
mitigation lever, quantified.

## 5. Notes, limitations and applicability

1. **Baffled-plane radiation.** The hull is idealised as a planar baffle. Hull
   curvature, sea-surface/ice-cover reflection, refraction and the water column
   waveguide are ignored, so the far-field pressure is a free-field baffle value.
   Grazing directions are excluded from the assessment fan (the baffle model is not
   valid there).
2. **Coherent-radiation upper bound.** A fully coherent hull surface radiates more
   directionally than a real hull: the directional maximum exceeds the omni level by
   11.8-14.5 dB. The propagated level therefore uses the omni-directional
   equivalent level; the directional maximum should be read as an upper bound.
3. **Truncated modal basis.** Three hull-girder modes are retained; the acoustic band
   is limited to `[band_min, 2 f_3] = [0.45, 7.08] Hz`
   because a 3-mode basis cannot represent the surface velocity above that band.
   Band coverage of the modal-velocity mean square is 99.5 %, 100.0 %, 99.9 %. The high-frequency (cavitation, local crushing, bubble) part of icebreaking
   noise is **outside** this model and is not claimed.
4. **Modal radiation superposition.** The acoustic power is built from per-mode
   radiation resistances; at the modal peaks the exact Rayleigh power of the full
   surface velocity deviates by up to 44 % (cross-modal
   interference). This is a declared model uncertainty of about +/-0.9 dB
   on the band power.
5. **ISO 19906 digits are WITHHELD.** The standard requires the ice-induced vibration
   assessment and refers the comfort limits to ISO 6897/ISO 10137 practice; its
   numeric table values could not be verified against a controlled copy inside this
   task, so only the documented curve *shape* is implemented and the plateau is a
   declared parameter with a sensitivity band. ISO 19906 contains **no** underwater
   SPL limit (NOT_SUPPORTED); the SPL envelope used here is the level the hull would
   radiate if its acceleration followed the vibration envelope.
6. **Force model.** The crushing force is `p_c h w_c` with a local contact pressure;
   the amplitude is a scenario parameter, not a calibrated ice-load model. The chain
   is linear in the force, so the results scale exactly with it (criterion C4).
7. **No structural FE model.** The hull girder is the equivalent uniform beam;
   `modal_basis_from_fem` exists to inject a ship-specific extraction when available.

band levels). The computed omni-directional source level of 129.4-182.3 dB re 1 uPa m falls inside the published icebreaking
range, which is a consistency check, not a validation.
